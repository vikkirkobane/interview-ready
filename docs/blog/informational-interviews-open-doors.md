# Why Informational Interviews Open Doors a Job Application Never Will

When you’re scrolling through job boards, polishing your résumé, and sending out applications, it’s easy to feel stuck in a loop: apply, wait, reject (or silence), repeat. Yet many African professionals discover that the most powerful career lever isn’t a polished cover letter—it’s a 15‑minute conversation. As career coaches often remind us, **informational interviews can open doors a job application never will**【https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/interviewing/informational-interview-guide】. A single short chat can bring clarity about a role, forge a genuine connection, and surface opportunities you never knew existed. Sometimes the best career move starts simply by picking up the phone and asking the right questions.

In this guide, we’ll break down what informational interviews are, why they outperform traditional applications, how to request and run them effectively, and how to turn the insights you gain into real‑world career moves—tailored for professionals across Africa.

## What Exactly Is an Informational Interview?

An informational interview is a **short, informal conversation** (typically 15‑30 minutes) with someone who works in a role, industry, or company you’re curious about【https://www.thestrategicstudent.com/strategy-guides/informational-interview-guide】. Unlike a job interview, your goal isn’t to ask for a position; it’s to learn. You’re saying, *“I want to understand your experience and gather advice,”* not *“Can you hire me?”* This distinction makes the exchange low‑pressure for both parties and often leads to candid, valuable insights.

Because the conversation is exploratory, you can speak with people at various levels—from recent alumni to senior leaders—without the formality of a hiring process. The informality encourages storytelling, which helps you grasp the day‑to‑day realities, unspoken challenges, and hidden perks of a career path.

## Why Informational Interviews Beat a Standard Job Application

1. **You Get Unfiltered, Real‑World Insight**  
   Job descriptions are marketing copy; they list desired qualifications but rarely reveal the actual workload, team dynamics, or growth trajectory. In an informational interview you can ask: *“What does a typical Tuesday look like for you?”* or *“What surprised you most when you started?”*【https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/interviewing/what-to-expect-in-informational-interview】. The answers give you a realistic preview that helps you decide whether a role truly fits your skills and aspirations.

2. **You Build a Genuine Connection Before You Need Anything**  
   Networking that feels transactional—reaching out only when you need a referral—often falls flat. By initiating a conversation purely to learn, you show respect for the other person’s time and expertise. This goodwill makes them more inclined to remember you when a relevant opening appears, or even to proactively share leads.

3. **You Uncover Hidden Opportunities**  
   Many roles are filled through referrals or internal moves before they ever hit a public board. A 15‑minute chat can reveal upcoming projects, upcoming expansions, or even a need for a skill set you possess but hadn’t considered marketing. As one career coach put it, *“Sometimes the best career move starts by simply picking up the phone and asking the right questions.”*  

4. **You Gain Clarity Faster Than Trial‑and‑Error**  
   Instead of spending months in a role that isn’t a fit, you can quickly validate (or rule out) a career direction. This saves time, reduces frustration, and lets you focus your job‑search energy on paths that genuinely excite you.

## How to Request an Informational Interview (The Right Way)

A thoughtful request dramatically increases your chances of a “yes.” Follow these steps:

1. **Identify the Right Person**  
   Look for alumni from your university, professionals in your target industry on LinkedIn, or speakers at recent webinars. If you’re aiming for a specific company, search for employees whose titles align with the role you’re exploring.

2. **Craft a Short, Personalized Message**  
   Keep it under 150 words. Mention how you found them, why you’re interested in their path, and request a brief 15‑20 minute conversation. Example:

   > *Hi [Name],*  
   > *I’m a [your current role/recent graduate] interested in [industry/role]. I came across your profile while researching [specific project/company initiative] and was impressed by your experience in [specific skill]. Would you be open to a 15‑minute call next week to hear about your career journey and any advice you’d share? I’m flexible and happy to work around your schedule.*  
   > *Thank you,*  
   > *[Your Name]*

   This approach respects their time, shows you’ve done homework, and makes the ask easy to say yes to【https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/blog/2024/02/01/the-power-of-informational-interviews-how-to-use-them-to-your-advantage/】.

3. **Offer Flexibility**  
   Propose a few time slots and note you can accommodate their timezone—a crucial consideration when connecting across African regions or with diaspora professionals.

4. **Follow Up Politely**  
   If you don’t hear back within 4‑5 days, send a short, friendly reminder. Persistence (without pressure) often pays off.

## Preparing for the Conversation

Preparation is the difference between a forgettable chat and a game‑changing one.

- **Research the Person and Their Company**  
  Scan their LinkedIn, recent articles, or company news. Knowing a recent project they led lets you ask insightful questions like, *“I saw you launched X initiative—what were the biggest lessons learned?”*【https://www.thestrategicstudent.com/strategy-guides/informational-interview-guide】.

- **Draft 5‑7 Open‑Ended Questions**  
  Focus on learning, not self‑promotion. Good starters include:
  - *Can you walk me through how you got into this field?*
  - *What skills have proven most valuable in your role?*
  - *What are the biggest challenges you face day‑to‑day?*
  - *How does your team typically approach [specific problem relevant to your interest]?*
  - *Is there anything you wish you knew when you were at my stage?*

- **Plan Logistics**  
  Confirm whether it’ll be a phone call, Zoom, or in‑person coffee. Test your tech beforehand, choose a quiet spot, and have a notebook or digital doc ready for notes.

## Conducting the Interview: Make Every Minute Count

During the call, keep these best practices in mind:

1. **Start with Gratitude and a Brief Intro**  
   Thank them for their time, share a 30‑second background about yourself, and restate that you’re seeking advice, not a job.

2. **Let Them Do Most of the Talking**  
   Aim for a 70/30 split (them talking, you listening). Use active listening cues—nodding, brief verbal affirmations—to encourage openness.

3. **Ask Follow‑Up Questions**  
   If they mention a challenge, dig deeper: *“How did you approach overcoming that?”* This shows genuine interest and often uncovers actionable tips.

4. **Respect Their Time**  
   Keep an eye on the clock. If you’ve reached the agreed limit, wrap up gracefully unless they offer to continue.

5. **End with Two Powerful Questions**  
   - *“Is there anyone else you’d recommend I speak with to learn more about this field?”* This expands your network organically.  
   - *“May I stay in touch and update you on my progress?”* This leaves the door open for future mentorship.

## Turning Insights into Action

The real value of an informational interview lies in what you do afterward.

- **Synthesize Your Notes**  
  Within 24 hours, write a brief summary: key takeaways, surprising facts, action items, and any new contacts promised.

- **Identify Skill Gaps**  
  If the interviewee highlighted a competency you lack (e.g., proficiency in a specific software or a certification), add it to your learning plan. Free or low‑cost platforms like Coursera, edX, and Google’s Career Certificates are accessible across many African nations.

- **Update Your Outreach Materials**  
  Use the language and insights gained to tweak your LinkedIn summary, résumé bullet points, or cover letter. For example, if you learned that “stakeholder management” is crucial in your target role, showcase any relevant experience you have—even if it came from volunteer work or a university project.

- **Follow Up with a Thank‑You Note**  
  Send a short email or LinkedIn message thanking them again, mentioning one specific piece of advice you found valuable, and reiterating your interest in staying connected. This small gesture strengthens the relationship.

- **Track Your Progress**  
  Keep a simple spreadsheet: date, person, company, key insights, follow‑up actions, and outcomes (e.g., “Applied to X role after learning about Y referral program”). Over time you’ll see patterns—perhaps certain industries consistently mention a need for data analysis, guiding your upskilling focus.

## Quick Tips for African Professionals

- **Leverage Alumni Networks**  
  Many African universities have active alumni groups on LinkedIn or Facebook. These communities are often eager to help fellow graduates.

- **Consider Time‑Zone Differences**  
  When reaching out to professionals abroad, propose windows that suit both parties (e.g., early morning your time, late afternoon theirs).

- **Highlight Remote‑Work Experience**  
  With the rise of global remote roles, emphasize any experience you have working across time zones or with international teams—this is a strong talking point in informational interviews.

- **Use Free Local Resources**  
  Organizations like the African Development Bank’s “Jobs for Youth in Africa” initiative, country‑specific career centers, and NGOs often host webinars where you can meet professionals willing to chat.

- **Stay Persistent but Polite**  
  If a professional declines or doesn’t respond, thank them for their consideration and move on. The goal is to build a network of genuine advocates, not to burn bridges.

## Takeaway

Informational interviews flip the traditional job‑search script: instead of waiting for a recruiter to notice your application, you proactively seek the people who already hold the roles you aspire to. A focused 15‑minute conversation can deliver the clarity, connections, and hidden opportunities that dozens of applications might never reveal. By mastering the art of the informational interview—requesting respectfully, preparing thoughtfully, listening actively, and acting on insights—you turn every chat into a stepping stone toward your next career move.
